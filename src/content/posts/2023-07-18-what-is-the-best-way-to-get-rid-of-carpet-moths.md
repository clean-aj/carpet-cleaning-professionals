## Try This to Remove Your Issues with Carpet Moth Infestations

Carpet moths were once quite a rare problem in the UK, but recent decades have seen a big rise in the number of carpet moths. These critters can make a nasty habit of chewing through our expensive carpets, textiles and upholstery. When you start to notice the presence of carpet moths, or parts of your carpet that look bare or dull, you might have a **carpet moth** problem.

The sooner you can take care of this, the better. Carpet moths will not leave on their own: not when there is so much textile to eat through. As they breed and pupate, this creates larvae all around your carpet that feeds on the fibres. Naturally, then, taking action and killing off a carpet moth infestation is a priority.

If you are having difficulty getting rid of carpet moths, then you should try the following solution. In most cases, this should eliminate these pests once and for all. If the carpet moths do not die off by using this method, it might be wise to contact a professional carpet cleaning service like ours.

### How to Eradicate a Carpet Moth Infestation

You’ll need to get started by vacuuming the carpet as thoroughly as possible. Go around every inch of your carpet, and give each area several runs over. This improves the chance of getting rid of larvae and eggs that might be buried in the carpet. 

As you do this, make sure that you are extremely thorough around your baseboards. These are the most common places for carpet moths to hide when they are not directly on the carpet. Please keep a lookout for carpet moths on darker parts of your walls and/or under furniture: they thrive in darkness, after all.

You can also invest in some moth traps. These are laid around on the carpet and basically act as a means of capturing the moths so that they are unable to give birth to any of their brood. You can also find that repeatedly spraying anti-moth carpet treatments on the carpet can be a good way to chase away a carpet moth problem.

Moth papers are also useful to invest in, as you can use these to help stop the breeding cycle. The main problem with carpet moths is that you might get rid of them, but they have a nasty habit of reappearing because you did not stop them from breeding. Moth papers can stop them from breeding entirely. 

The main thing to do, though, is to look for a carpet that is mothproof in the first place. Many wool carpets arrive with mothproofing already included. This is a good way to help make sure that you do not need to deal with this problem in the first place. For already fitted carpets, though, could you try out the above suggestion?
