## Understanding Carpet Moths in Surrey
Carpet moths, such as *Tinea pellionella* (case-bearing clothes moth) and *Trichophaga tapetzella* (carpet moth), are small but mighty foes. The adult moths—tiny at 6-8mm with golden-brown wings—don’t cause the damage; it’s their larvae that do. These cream-coloured grubs feed on keratin, a protein in wool, silk, and hair, targeting your Surrey home’s natural-fibre carpets and furnishings.

Surrey’s climate plays right into their hands. With mild winters and humid summers, plus the warmth of central heating in homes from Weybridge to Woking, carpet moths thrive. Whether you’re in a Georgian house in Godalming or a modern flat in Epsom, these pests can settle in unnoticed.

## The Carpet Moth Life Cycle
Knowing how carpet moths operate is the first step to stopping them. Adult females lay 40-200 eggs in dark, quiet spots—under your Surrey pine dresser, behind curtains, or in carpet corners. These eggs hatch in 4-10 days, unleashing larvae that feed for weeks to years, depending on conditions like humidity and food supply. After pupating in cocoons, they emerge as adults to start again.

In Surrey’s older properties, with their hidden nooks and crannies, this cycle can go undetected until your prized rug shows holes. That’s why vigilance is essential.

## Spotting Carpet Moths in Surrey
Catching an infestation early can save your carpets. Look for small holes in rugs or carpets, often with sandy frass (larval waste) nearby. Silken tubes or cases might appear, especially in historic homes in Dorking or Leatherhead. Adult moths may flutter briefly or dart into shadows.
