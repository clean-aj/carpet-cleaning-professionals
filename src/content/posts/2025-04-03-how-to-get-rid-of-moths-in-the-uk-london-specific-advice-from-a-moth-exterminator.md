## Why Moths Are a UK Problem—And Worse in London
Moths thrive across the UK, but London’s unique conditions make it a hotspot. The city’s damp climate, dense housing, and abundance of natural fibres like wool and cotton create a moth paradise. A [Telegraph article](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/news/2019/06/17/moth-invasion-uk-homes-rise-experts-warn/) reported a rise in moth infestations nationwide, with urban areas like London hit hardest due to tightly packed homes and textile-rich interiors.

Adult moths don’t eat—it’s their larvae that wreak havoc, feeding on carpets, clothes, and upholstery. In London, where historic properties meet modern flats, moths find plenty of opportunities. Let’s explore how to spot and banish them.

### Common UK Moths in London Homes
In London, two moth types dominate: clothes moths (Tineola bisselliella), which target wardrobes, and carpet moths (also Tineola bisselliella), which attack floor coverings. Case-bearing moths (Tinea pellionella) also pop up, hitting both clothes and upholstery. ThermoPest’s moth info page online can help you tell them apart. Knowing your moth is key to getting rid of it.

## Signs of Moths in Your London Home
Spotting moths early can save you from costly damage. Here’s what to look for:

### Clothes Moth Clues
- **Holes in Fabrics:** Small, irregular holes in wool, silk, or cashmere garments signal clothes moth larvae.
- **Silken Webs:** Fine webbing or tubes in wardrobes or drawers are a giveaway.

### Carpet Moth Signs
- **Bald Patches:** Threadbare spots on carpets or rugs mean carpet moth larvae are feeding.
- **Silky Threads:** Check under furniture for silk-like webbing left by larvae.

### Adult Moth Activity
- **Small Moths:** Clothes moths are golden-brown, while carpet moths are beige—both are tiny and active in dim light.

If you’re seeing these signs, it’s time to act—moths spread fast in London’s close-knit homes.

## DIY Methods to Get Rid of Moths in the UK
Caught moths early? You might try some DIY fixes tailored to London homes. These can help, but they’re rarely a full solution. Here’s what to do:

### Clean and Vacuum
- **Wash Clothes:** Wash affected garments at a high temperature (if safe) or dry clean them to kill eggs and larvae.
- **Vacuum Thoroughly:** Hit carpets, rugs, and wardrobes to remove larvae and eggs, then empty the vacuum outside.

### Freeze Treatment
For delicate items or small rugs, seal them in a plastic bag and freeze for 48 hours. The cold eliminates moths without damage.

### Repellents
Lavender sachets, cedar blocks, or rosemary oil can deter adult moths. Place them in wardrobes or near carpets, but they won’t stop an active infestation.

### Traps
Pheromone traps catch adult male moths, cutting breeding. Available at UK hardware stores, they won’t tackle larvae already present.

For more on moth behaviour, the [Wikipedia page on clothes moths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tineola_bisselliella) offers a solid overview. These steps might reduce moths, but in London’s busy urban setting, they often fall short.

## How Moths Get into London Homes (and How to Stop Them)
Moths don’t appear by magic—here’s what brings them in and how to keep them out:

### Entry Points in London
- **Open Windows:** Adult moths fly in during the UK’s warmer months, especially summer.
- **Second-Hand Items:** Vintage rugs or clothes from London markets can carry eggs.
- **Neighbouring Properties:** In flats or terraces, moths can migrate from nearby homes.

### Prevention Tips for Londoners
Once moths are gone, protect your home with these steps:

- **Store Smart:** Use airtight containers for clothes and vacuum-seal rugs when not in use.
- **Check Purchases:** Clean second-hand finds before bringing them indoors.
- **Block Access:** Fit screens or draught excluders to stop moths flying in.
