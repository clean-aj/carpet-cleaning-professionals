## Why Moths Are a Problem in London Homes
London’s unique blend of historic homes, modern flats, and humid climate creates the perfect breeding ground for moths. The city’s dense population and abundance of textiles—like carpets, curtains, and clothing—provide ample food sources for moth larvae. According to the [British Pest Control Association (BPCA)](https://bpca.org.uk/), moths are among the most common household pests in the UK, with carpet moths and clothes moths being particularly troublesome in urban areas like London.

Moths don’t just flutter around lights; their larvae are the real culprits, feeding on natural fibres such as wool, silk, and cotton. If you’ve noticed small holes in your favourite jumper or bald patches on your carpet, it’s time to take action. Let’s dive into the types of moths you might encounter and how to tackle them.

### Common Types of Moths in London
Before you can get rid of moths, it’s helpful to know what you’re dealing with. In London, two species dominate household infestations:

- **Case-Bearing Moths (Tinea pellionella):** These pests create small, silken cases that their larvae carry around, often found in wardrobes or on soft furnishings. They target clothing and upholstery.
- **Carpet Moths and Clothes Moths (Tineola bisselliella):** These are the textile destroyers, with larvae that feast on carpets, rugs, and clothing.

## How to Spot a Moth Infestation
Catching a moth problem early can save you time, money, and frustration. Here are the key signs to watch for in your London home:

### Signs of Carpet Moths
- **Bald Patches on Carpets:** If your rugs or carpets look threadbare in spots, moth larvae could be to blame.
- **Silk Webbing:** Look for fine, silky threads under furniture or along carpet edges.
- **Small Moths Flying Around:** Adult carpet moths are small, beige, and often seen near infested areas.

### Signs of Case-Bearing Moths
- **Holes in Clothing:** Tiny, irregular holes in wool, cashmere, or silk garments are a telltale sign.
- **Silken Cases:** Check wardrobes for small, tube-like cases made of silk and fabric fibres, often with larvae inside.
- **Fluttering Adults:** Case-bearing moths are small and mottled, preferring dark, undisturbed areas.

## DIY Methods to Get Rid of Moths
If you’ve caught the problem early, you might be tempted to try some DIY solutions. While these can help with minor infestations, they’re often not enough for a full-blown moth takeover. Here’s what you can do:

### Cleaning and Vacuuming
Start by thoroughly vacuuming carpets, rugs, and upholstery to remove larvae and eggs. Pay special attention to dark corners and under furniture where moths like to hide. Wash affected clothing at a high temperature (if the fabric allows) or dry clean it to kill any eggs.

### Natural Repellents
Cedarwood blocks, lavender sachets, and essential oils like rosemary can deter adult moths. However, these won’t eliminate an existing infestation—they’re better for prevention. Place them in wardrobes or drawers to keep moths at bay.

### Traps
Pheromone traps can catch adult male moths, reducing their ability to reproduce. You can find these in hardware stores, but they won’t address larvae already munching through your belongings.
