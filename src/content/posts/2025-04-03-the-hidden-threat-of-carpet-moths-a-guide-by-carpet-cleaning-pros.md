## What Are Carpet Moths?
Carpet moths belong to a group of insects known as pantry and clothes moths, specifically the species *Tinea pellionella* (case-bearing clothes moth) and *Trichophaga tapetzella* (carpet moth). Despite their name, they’re not the adult moths fluttering around your home that cause the damage—it’s their larvae. These tiny, cream-coloured grubs have a voracious appetite for natural fibres, particularly keratin, a protein found in wool, silk, and even animal hair. This makes your woollen carpets, rugs, and upholstery prime targets.

In the UK, carpet moths thrive in our temperate climate, especially in homes with central heating that provides the warmth and humidity they love. Unlike pantry moths, which feast on dried goods like grains, carpet moths are drawn to the organic materials in your furnishings. The adult moths are small—about 6-8mm long—with golden-brown wings, and they’re notoriously difficult to spot as they prefer dark, undisturbed corners.

## The Life Cycle of Carpet Moths
Understanding the life cycle of carpet moths is key to tackling an infestation. It begins when an adult female lays between 40 and 200 eggs, often in hidden spots like under furniture, along skirting boards, or deep within carpet piles. These eggs hatch within 4-10 days, depending on temperature, into larvae that immediately start feeding. The larval stage can last anywhere from a month to two years, depending on conditions like food availability and humidity. Once fully fed, the larvae pupate into cocoons before emerging as adults, ready to start the cycle again.

This prolonged larval stage is why carpet moths can cause such extensive damage before you even notice them. By the time you spot a moth fluttering around, the real culprits—the larvae—may have already chewed through your favourite rug.

## Why Are Carpet Moths a Problem?
For homeowners and renters alike, carpet moths pose a significant threat to both aesthetics and finances. A wool carpet or an heirloom rug isn’t just a floor covering; it’s an investment. The larvae don’t discriminate, gnawing irregular holes and leaving behind a trail of destruction that can ruin the look and value of your furnishings. In severe cases, entire sections of carpet may need replacing—a costly fix that could have been avoided with early intervention.

Beyond carpets, these pests can also target curtains, upholstered furniture, and even clothing stored near the floor. If you’ve got a mix of synthetic and natural fibres in your carpet, they’ll zero in on the wool or silk components, leaving unsightly bald patches. For British households, where wool carpets remain a popular choice for their durability and warmth, carpet moths are an all-too-common nuisance.

Spotting an infestation early can save you a lot of trouble. So, what should you look out for? The telltale signs include small, irregular holes in your carpet or rugs, often accompanied by fine, sandy-looking debris known as frass (larval excrement). You might also notice silken tubes or cases—especially with the case-bearing clothes moth—where larvae live and feed. Adult moths may be seen flying erratically, though they’re more likely to scuttle away from light. ## How Do Carpet Moths Get into Your Home?
You might wonder how these unwelcome guests find their way into your house. The truth is, they’re opportunistic. Adult moths can fly in through open windows or doors, especially during warmer months. They might also hitch a ride on second-hand furniture, rugs, or clothing bought from charity shops or online marketplaces. Once inside, they seek out dark, undisturbed areas to lay their eggs—think under your sofa, behind wardrobes, or in rarely vacuumed corners.

Poor housekeeping can exacerbate the problem. Dust, pet hair, and human skin cells accumulating in carpets provide extra nourishment for larvae, making your home even more appealing. That’s where regular maintenance from a professional outfit like Carpet Cleaning Pros comes in handy.

## Preventing Carpet Moth Infestations
Prevention is always better than cure, and there are steps you can take to keep carpet moths at bay:

1. **Regular Vacuuming**: Hoover your carpets, rugs, and upholstery at least once a week, paying special attention to edges and under furniture. This removes eggs and larvae before they can hatch or mature.
2. **Declutter**: Moths love undisturbed areas, so keep your floors clear and move furniture occasionally to disrupt their hiding spots.
3. **Store Wisely**: If you’ve got wool rugs or textiles in storage, use sealed plastic bags or containers to keep moths out.
4. **Ventilation**: Reduce humidity by airing out rooms, as moths thrive in damp conditions.
5. **Professional Cleaning**: A deep clean from Carpet Cleaning Pros not only refreshes your carpets but also eliminates any lurking eggs or larvae.
